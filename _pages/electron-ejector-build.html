Build an Electron Ejector

Everything you need to build your own: parts, steps, and wiring.

The build uses a Raspberry Pi 4B, addressable LED strips, plywood, CLS timber, and 3D-printed parts. The print files and Python code are on GitHub. Our electronics were fully enclosed and PAT tested.

Steps

  1. Cut all CLS pieces to size.
  2. Do NOT pre-drill holes in the Mid Brace of Tower Support (Back) yet. These need to be drilled with the tower so everything lines up.
  3. Screw the Tower Support CLS sections together at the red marked locations on each Tower Foot with 4 mm × 70 mm woodscrews. Use clamps to help keep things square. (Figures 1-2)
  4. Cut all plywood sheets to size.
  5. Pre-drill the electronics holes in the Support Platform and Front Panel, but NOT the Tower Bolt Holes in the Support Platform. These need to be drilled with the tower so everything lines up (Figures 3-4).
  6. Use 4 mm × 15 mm woodscrews to attach all the panels to the Tower Support frame, at the red marked locations, on all the panels. Make sure to avoid the existing screws in the frame Tower Feet just to the inside of the Frame screws (Figures 5-6).
  7. Lightly sand down any sharp edges.
  8. Paint the outside of the Tower Support Box.

Steps

  1. Attach the Tower Top to the Tower Bottom by aligning the Mid-Cross braces and Front Panels. Use the included mounting screws to attach the hinge in its open position (Figure 19).

  1. Cut LED strips into 1 m lengths (if needed). Solder the JST connectors between the sections, according to the wiring diagram.
  2. Using the Mount Glue Clamp, take the Diffuser Mounts and place them according to the diagram in Figure 28, starting with the bottom of the LED strips at the right hand side of the diagram:
  3. Use the Silicone Glue and Mount Glue Clamp to fix the strips to each Mount, and let them fully cure. Check the spacing against the LED Diffuser Covers, and adjust if needed (Figures 29-30).
  4. Place an M4 nut in each of the Diffuser Mounts, an M4 Bolt through the outer hole and screw it into the nut. Use a dab of Silicone glue to seal the slot and trap each nut (Figure 31). Let this cure fully.

Take care the LED strips are in the channels of the Top Diffuser Cover so they don’t get pinched. Press firmly to stick the Velcro to the tower. (Figure 32)

Steps

  1. Paint the Button Box parts, if desired.
  2. Place ‘Hit’ Arcade button through the Button Box Top, and screw plastic nut on back to clamp it to the Top.
  3. Cut the length of 3-strand wire needed to have the Button Box placed where you like (at least 1.5 m). Strip and terminate the 3 wire ends with blade terminals, and attach these to the button according to the wiring diagram.
  4. Screw the Top to the Body with M4 bolts.
  5. Make sure the wire lines up in the hole of the Box Body so it doesn’t get pinched, and screw the Bottom to the Body with M4 bolts.
  6. Run the 3-strand wire from the Wire Hole Grommet beneath the LED Diffuser and up through the Grommet inside the Tower (Figure 33).
  7. Paint the Hammers to match the Barrel Jack Panel Mounts.
  8. Use super glue to fix the caps onto the hammers.
  9. Short the Barrel Jack Plugs by placing a small length of Single-strand wire to link the +ve and -ve terminals of the plug.
  10. Cut a length of String/Yarn to desired size (at least 1 m), and feed it into the back of the Barrel Jack Hammer Plug 3D printed part, knot it inside.
  11. Place the Barrel Jack Plug electronic part inside the 3D printed housing and screw the housing to the faceplate (Figure 34).

Steps

  1. Referencing the Electronics Diagram in the electronics diagram, wire the Female Barrel jack plugs with 2 or 3-strand wire.
    1. Cut each to length so it can be fed up through the Tower Wire Grommet and to the top of the Tower, where the Raspberry Pi will sit; approx. 1.5 m each.
    2. Strip the wire ends, and attach a Bootlace terminal to each end, then attach to Barrel Jack screw terminals.
    3. With wires attached, unscrew the plastic nut on the back of the Hammer Plug Panel Mounts, remove the side plate, and place the Female Barrel Jack Plug inside.
    4. Replace the side plate, and tighten the plastic nut to trap the wired Barrel Jack.
  2. Referencing the Electronics Diagram in the electronics diagram, wire the ‘Select’ Arcade button 3-strand wire.
    1. Cut each to length so it can be fed up through the Tower Wire Grommet and to the top of the Tower, where the Raspberry Pi will sit; approx. 1.5 m.
    2. Strip the wire ends, and attach a Blade terminal to each end, then attach to the Arcade Button wiring blades.
  3. Referencing the Electronics Diagram in the electronics diagram, prepare a wiring harness for the LEDs:
    1. Use a Female Barrel Jack connector (matching the LED Power Supply).
    2. Place a 1000 µF capacitor across the +ve and -ve sides of the Barrel Jack
    3. Cut a short length of wire each for the +ve and -ve connections for power. Terminate the ends with a Bootlace connector.
    4. Strip a short section in the middle of each wire to splice in the JST wires.
    5. Solder the Female JST +ve and -ve to these wires as a splice.
    6. Wrap each splice in electrical tape or heat shrink.
    7. Cut the Data JST wire, and solder a 500 Ω resistor in the middle, reattaching the Data wire with the resistor in the middle of the cut. Heat shrink or electrical tape the join to protect the resistor.
  4. Route all cables according to the simplified wiring diagram in Figure 35.
    1. Red = Hit Box 3-strand Wire
    2. Yellow = Select Button 3-strand Wire
    3. White, Dark Blue, Orange = Hammer Female Barrel Jack Plugs
    4. Green = Screen HDMI
    5. Beige = LED Data and Power
    6. Light Blue = LED Power Supply
    7. Purple = Screen Power Supply
    8. Grey = Raspberry Pi Power Supply
  5. Attach the Screw Terminal Header to the Raspberry Pi.
  6. Screw the Raspberry Pi to the Electronics Mounting Plate.
  7. Screw the 12 V Voltage Regulator to the Electronics Mounting Plate, and attach the cover with zip ties.
  8. Attach the Electronics Mounting Plate to the back of the Tower with Velcro Strips.
  9. Zip tie the Arcade Button and Hammer Plug wires to the Electronics Mounting plate on the printed pedestals.
  10. Terminate all wire ends to be plugged into Raspberry Pi screw terminal header with Bootlace ferrules.
  11. Perform the detailed wiring to the Raspberry Pi and the 12 V Step-Up/Step-Down Regulator per the Electronics Diagram in the electronics diagram, but ensure NO POWER is plugged in.
  12. Double-check the wiring, then plug into power and turn on the Raspberry Pi.
  13. Attach a keyboard and mouse to complete the setup.
  14. Download and run the Electron Ejector code from GitHub.
